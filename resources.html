FAQ

Common questions about the assessment process.

What's the difference between an LCA and an EPD?

A Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) is the underlying technical study that quantifies environmental impacts across a product's life cycle. An Environmental Product Declaration (EPD) is a standardized, publishable document that reports the results of an LCA in a format defined by a program operator — it's the public-facing output, not the study itself.

How long does a Life Cycle Assessment take?

Most single-product LCAs take 8–12 weeks from data collection to final report, depending on supply chain complexity and how much primary data you already have available. Multi-product or first-time programs typically take longer.

Do I need critical review for my EPD?

Most EPD program operators require independent critical review before publication, and any study supporting a public comparative assertion needs it under ISO 14044. We can tell you what your specific program operator requires during scoping.

What standards do you follow?

Every study follows ISO 14040 and 14044 for LCA methodology, and ISO 14025 for EPDs. Depending on your goals, we also work within GHG Protocol, CSRD/ESRS, and specific program operator requirements (UL, NSF, SCS, ASTM).

What data do you need from us to get started?

At minimum, a bill of materials and information on your manufacturing process and energy use. The more primary data you can provide — supplier-specific energy and material data — the less we rely on industry-average datasets, which strengthens the study.

Standards library

The frameworks behind our studies, explained plainly.

ISO 14040

LCA Principles & Framework

Defines the goal, scope, and overall structure a life cycle assessment must follow.

ISO 14044

LCA Requirements & Guidelines

The detailed technical requirements for inventory analysis, impact assessment, and interpretation.

ISO 14025

Environmental Labels — Type III

Governs how EPDs are structured, verified, and published under a program operator.

ISO 14071

Critical Review Processes

Sets requirements for independent critical review, including reviewer qualifications and panel review.

GHG Protocol

Corporate & Product Standards

The most widely used framework for greenhouse gas accounting, covering Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions.

CSRD / ESRS

EU Sustainability Reporting

The EU's corporate sustainability disclosure requirements, including double materiality assessment.
